Timeline restructuring — three visual proposals

Rendered in YatraFlow's own design language (Sora / Inter, CTI palette) with the demo trip's real stops, so you're judging the structure, not a wireframe. Nothing here is implemented yet — this is the decision document.

1336lines in TimelineTab.tsx — five components in one file
17useState hooks live at the top of that file
19props on the DaySection component alone
What "too complicated" looks like structurally: every day renders at once — journey clocks, travel legs, fuel estimates, a spark chart, warnings, weather, nearby-POI chips, "Continue to X" chips, a long-ride halt planner, collapse/rename/copy controls, day-start times, status toggles. A 6-day trip is six walls of UI before you've scrolled to Day 3. The three proposals below attack that from different angles and can be combined.
1

Collapse days by default — "the day rail"

Every day becomes one scannable row: where you go, what it costs, and whether anything needs attention. The collapse machinery already exists (loadDayCollapsed) — this proposal flips the default and redesigns what a collapsed day says. Click to open the one day you're actually working on.

The route line is the summary. Stop names joined by arrows — you recognise your day by its shape, not by reading six cards. Muted kind-tags ("drive day", "stay day") replace the travel panels that used to sit inside every day.
One number, one spark. Stops · km · ₹ and a mini load-bar per day. The amber bar segment is the busiest stop — a load indicator you can read without opening anything.
Warnings surface here, not buried inside. "Over-packed", "arrive after closing" — the two things you actually need to act on — promote to the row itself. Today they're interleaved between stops where you discover them by accident.

Per-stop actions appear on hover/focus only. Edit and delete sit at the right edge of each row and fade in — visible when you're editing, invisible when you're reading. On touch they're always on. This is the same pattern the board uses.
Legs compress to one line — "12 km · 28 min" between rows. Fuel estimates, mode detail, and the leg's source move into the Inspect view (Proposal 2). The planning question — "how far apart are these?" — needs six words, not a panel.
2

Split "Plan" from "Inspect" — one view, two modes

Same timeline, same data, two reading intentions. Plan is for changing things — minimal chrome, big targets, impact preview on every change. Inspect is for studying the plan — clocks, fuel, arrival math, warnings, everything dense and none of it editable. The toggle sits where the collapse controls live today; the mode persists per user (localStorage, like theme).

Density becomes a feature, not a bug. Inspect mode keeps everything the Timeline has today — nothing is lost, it just stops interrupting the act of editing. The people who want the full picture can have exactly the view they had before.
Editing tools disappear in Inspect. No drag handles, no delete buttons, no add zones — you can't fat-finger a delete while reading your arrival times. This also makes the Timeline safe to share/read on a phone during the trip itself.
3

Evict the specialist tools — "Long ride" becomes a collapsible panel

The halt planner (choose break stops at chosen kilometres of a long drive) is a power feature that currently renders inline inside every day. It moves into its own collapsed panel at the top of the Timeline — one click away for the drive-day crowd, invisible for everyone else. Same for "Continue to X" and nearby-POI chips, which fold into the same panel.

Timeline header with the specialist panelcollapsed by default · opens only when a day needs it

Long-ride tools — Day 5 · Munnar → Alleppey

Breaks suggested by road distance, not by stop order. Click a row to add it as a halt; the impact preview shows the time cost.

km 42 Chinnakanal viewpoint — chai + stretch · ~15 min
km 96 Kumily junction — lunch stop · ~40 min · added ✓
km 140 Haripad temple square — chai · ~10 min
Same data, one home. The halt planner, "Continue to X", and nearby-POI chips all add stops — today they're three different UIs sprinkled through the timeline. Folding them into one labelled panel means one place to look for "help me fill this day".
The kilometre-based view is the right mental model for a drive day. You plan breaks by where they fall on the road, not by which stop comes fourth. Keeping this list ordered by km — not by insertion — is the actual feature.
Days without a long drive show nothing at all. The panel header only renders when the day's drive exceeds a threshold (say 100 km). A Munnar base-camp day never sees it.
+

How they combine — and what I'd do first

These aren't three competing designs; they stack. Here's the natural build order, cheapest-first, with the honest cost of each.

Step Ship Effort Risk
1 Day rail (Proposal 1) — flip the collapse default, build the summary row Small — state + one row component; collapse storage already exists Low · fully reversible
2 Evict specialist tools (Proposal 3) — halt planner + chips into one panel Medium — the planner component already exists, just needs a new home + threshold Low · a move, not a rewrite
3 Plan / Inspect toggle (Proposal 2) — needs 1+2 in place first to have clean things to toggle Larger — a genuine mode with prop threading; also the moment to split the 1336-line file into modules Medium · worth planning properly
Step 1 alone probably fixes 80% of the "complicated" feeling. Six days as six rows, warnings surfaced, one day open at a time. If we ship nothing else, ship this.
The file split can ride along with step 3 — when Plan/Inspect forces `TravelPanel`, `HaltPlanRow` and `DaySpark` behind a mode boundary anyway, extracting them into their own modules is nearly free. Doing it earlier would mean churning imports twice.
